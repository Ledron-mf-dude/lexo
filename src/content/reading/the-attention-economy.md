When a product is free, the saying goes, you are the product. In the case of most social media platforms, that is only half right. What is really being sold is not you but your attention — measured in seconds, packaged into audiences and auctioned to advertisers many times a day.

Seen in this light, many design decisions that seem harmless start to look rather different. The feed that never ends, the video that starts playing before you have chosen it, the red badge that appears on an icon: each of these features exists because it has been shown, through endless testing, to keep people looking at the screen a little longer. Engineers rarely set out to make anyone unhappy. They are simply rewarded for increasing a number, and the number they are asked to increase is time spent in the app.

The consequences are easy to underestimate because they are spread so thinly across the day. Having checked a phone, people typically take several minutes to return to the depth of concentration they had before. Multiply that by dozens of interruptions and an hour of focused work can quietly dissolve into a series of fragments. Hardly anyone notices this happening; it simply feels as though there is never quite enough time.

Critics argue that the problem cannot be solved by individuals alone, given that they are up against teams whose full-time job is to capture their attention. Some have called for regulation: limits on autoplay for children, for instance, or a requirement that apps show users how much time they have spent. Others are sceptical, pointing out that attention has always been fought over — by newspapers, by television, by advertising on every street corner.

Whatever one concludes about regulation, there are a few things individuals can do. Turning off all non-essential notifications removes a surprising number of interruptions at a stroke. Keeping the phone in another room while working, however inconvenient it may seem, is remarkably effective. And asking, before opening an app, what exactly one wants from it turns an automatic habit back into a choice.

## Граматика
- participle-clauses — *Seen in this light*, many design decisions… start to look rather different. — Пасивний дієприкметниковий зворот (*Seen…* = *If they are seen…*): коротко вводить точку зору.
- participle-clauses — *Having checked* a phone, people typically take several minutes to return… — Перфектний дієприкметник (*having* + V3): дія сталася перед головною.
- passive-advanced — each of these features exists because it *has been shown*… to keep people looking at the screen — Пасив із неособовою формою: *it has been shown to keep* — «було доведено, що вона утримує».
- complex-prepositions — *given that* they are up against teams… — *Given that* — «з огляду на те, що»: вводить причину, яку враховує висновок.
- wh-ever-words — *however inconvenient it may seem* — *However* + прикметник: «хоч яким незручним це може здаватися». Поступка.
