Predictions about artificial intelligence and work tend to swing between two extremes. In one version, machines will have taken over most office jobs within a decade; in the other, they are glorified autocomplete tools that will change very little. The reality emerging in workplaces that have adopted AI assistants is less dramatic than either, and in some ways more interesting.

What is changing first is not which jobs exist, but how the work within them is divided. Tasks that consist largely of producing a first draft — summarising a report, writing a standard email, turning notes into slides — are increasingly handed to software. What remains for the human is the part that was always harder to describe in a job advert: deciding what the document is for, judging whether the draft is actually right, and taking responsibility for the result.

This shift places a premium on a skill that is rarely taught explicitly: evaluation. An assistant can produce a plausible paragraph in seconds, but plausibility is not accuracy. Professionals who are good at spotting errors, missing context or subtle misrepresentations become more valuable, not less. Those who accept the output uncritically risk spreading mistakes faster than they ever could on their own.

There is a less obvious cost, too. Junior employees have traditionally learned their trade by doing exactly the routine tasks that are now being automated. If newcomers no longer write the first draft of anything, how will they develop the judgement needed to evaluate one? Some companies are already experimenting with deliberately keeping certain tasks manual for trainees — an approach that would have seemed absurdly inefficient only a few years ago.

By the end of the decade, most office workers will probably be spending part of every day working alongside such tools. Whether that turns out to be liberating or deskilling will depend less on the technology itself than on the choices organisations make about how to use it.

- future-continuous-perfect — machines *will have taken over* most office jobs within a decade — Future Perfect: дія завершиться до певного моменту в майбутньому.
