Look down on almost any large city from above and you will see a vast, grey landscape of roofs: millions of square metres of space that do nothing but keep the rain off. Over the past two decades, a growing number of architects and city planners have begun to ask whether that space could be put to better use. Their answer, increasingly, is green.

A green roof is, at its simplest, a layer of plants growing on a waterproof membrane. The benefits are considerable. In summer, the plants absorb heat that would otherwise be radiated back into the street, helping to reduce the so-called urban heat island effect, whereby city centres can be several degrees warmer than the surrounding countryside. During heavy rain, the soil holds back water that would otherwise rush into overloaded drains, lowering the risk of flash floods. Green roofs also provide a habitat for insects and birds and, not least, offer residents somewhere pleasant to sit.

Given these advantages, one might expect green roofs to be everywhere by now. They are not, and the reasons are largely practical. Installing one on an existing building requires an assessment of whether the structure can bear the additional weight, particularly when the soil is saturated. Costs are higher than for a conventional roof, and although the savings on heating and cooling are real, they take years to recover the investment. Maintenance, too, is often underestimated: a neglected roof garden quickly turns into a patch of weeds.

Cities that have made progress have generally done so through a combination of rules and rewards. Some require all new flat-roofed buildings above a certain size to include vegetation; others offer subsidies or reductions in water charges to owners who install it. The introduction of such policies has, in several cases, led to a rapid increase in green roof coverage within a decade.

Green roofs will not, on their own, solve the problems of urban heat or flooding. But the growing recognition that every surface in a city can serve more than one purpose may prove to be their most lasting contribution.

## Граматика
- nominalisation — *The introduction of such policies* has… led to *a rapid increase in green roof coverage*. — Номіналізація: дії (*introduce, increase*) стають іменниками. Типово для академічного й аналітичного стилю — щільно і безособово.
- relative-clauses-advanced — the so-called urban heat island effect, *whereby* city centres can be several degrees warmer — *Whereby* = «за якого / завдяки якому»: формальний сполучник, що пояснює механізм.
- conditionals-advanced — the plants absorb heat that *would otherwise be radiated* back into the street — *Would otherwise* — прихована умова: «яке інакше (якби не рослини) випромінювалося б».
- complex-prepositions — *Given these advantages*, one might expect green roofs to be everywhere by now. — *Given* + іменник: «з огляду на…». Формальна заміна *Because of*.
- seem — the growing recognition… *may prove to be* their most lasting contribution — *Prove to be* — «виявитися». Разом із *may* — обережний прогноз.
