Serhii's old phone is dying. The battery lasts only half a day, and the screen is broken. So on Saturday he went to an electronics shop to buy a new one.

The shop assistant showed him three phones. "This one is the cheapest," she said. "It's good for calls and messages, but the camera isn't very good. This one is more expensive, but it has a bigger screen and a better battery. And this one is the newest and the most powerful. It has the best camera in the shop."

"How much is it?" Serhii asked.

"Forty-five thousand hryvnias."

Serhii laughed. "That's more than my monthly salary!"

"Then you should think about what you really need," the assistant said. "Do you take a lot of photos? Do you play games?"

"Not really. I mostly use maps, messages and online banking. But I want a battery that lasts all day."

"In that case, you should take the middle one. It's not as fast as the newest model, but it's fast enough for you. And you shouldn't buy the cheapest one. Its battery is even worse than your old phone's."

Serhii took her advice. A month later, he is still happy. His battery lasts two days, and he has more money for a holiday.

## Граматика
- comparatives-superlatives — *the cheapest*… *more expensive*… *a bigger screen*… *the most powerful*… *the best camera* — Порівняння: короткі прикметники з -er / -est, довгі з *more / the most*, неправильні: *good → better → the best*, *bad → worse → the worst*.
- as-as — It's *not as fast as* the newest model. — *Not as … as* — «не такий…, як». Так ввічливіше сказати, що щось гірше.
- too-enough — it's fast *enough* for you — *Enough* стоїть після прикметника: *fast enough*, а не *enough fast*.
- modals-obligation — you *should take* the middle one… you *shouldn't buy* the cheapest one — *Should / shouldn't* — порада. Після *should* — дієслово без *to*.
