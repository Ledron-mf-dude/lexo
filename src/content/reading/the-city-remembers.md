Cities are often described as palimpsests: manuscripts scraped clean and written over, in which traces of earlier texts nonetheless show through. The metaphor is a tired one, but it endures because it captures something true. No city is ever wholly rebuilt. Each generation inherits the streets of the last, and its choices about what to keep, rename or erase are themselves a kind of writing.

The most obvious layer is the official one: monuments, plaques and street names, the city's attempts to tell its own story. These are also the most contested. A street renamed three times in a century is a record not of one history but of several, each superseding the last. Long after a name has been officially changed, the old one may persist in everyday speech, the habits of residents proving more durable than the decisions of councils.

Less deliberate, and in some ways more eloquent, are the marks the city did not intend to leave. The pockmarked stone of a facade, its scars carefully left unrepaired; the sudden gap in a row of houses where a building once stood; the tram tracks that surface through worn asphalt on a street where no tram has run for decades. None of these was designed to commemorate anything, which is precisely why they are so difficult to argue with.

And then there is memory that resides in people rather than in stone. The bakery everyone still calls by the name of its owner, who died thirty years ago; the corner where, by unspoken agreement, nobody parks; the route the older residents take to avoid a square that was, within living memory, a place of fear. This layer is the most fragile of all, vanishing as those who carry it do.

A city, then, remembers in at least three registers at once — the declared, the accidental and the lived. Its character arguably depends on the friction between them, and on its willingness to let the less convenient memories remain visible.

- absolute-clauses — the habits of residents *proving* more durable than the decisions of councils — Незалежний дієприкметниковий зворот із власним підметом (*the habits … proving*): = *because the habits prove…*
