Most of us treat sleep as something we can cut when life gets busy. We stay up to finish a project, scroll through our phones until midnight, and promise ourselves that we'll catch up at the weekend. Yet sleep scientists keep warning that this is one of the worst deals we can make.

Sleep is not simply a pause in our day. While we are asleep, the brain sorts through the information it has collected, deciding which memories to keep and which to throw away. People who sleep well after learning something new remember it significantly better than those who stay awake. In other words, if you study late into the night and then sleep for four hours, you are throwing away part of the work you have just done.

The brain also cleans itself at night. During deep sleep, fluid flows through brain tissue and washes away waste products that build up during the day. Researchers believe that this process, which works far less efficiently when we are awake, may help protect us from certain diseases later in life.

The effects of too little sleep appear quickly. After just one short night, people react more slowly, find it harder to control their emotions, and are more likely to reach for sugary food. A driver who has been awake for eighteen hours performs about as badly as one who has been drinking.

Unfortunately, the "weekend catch-up" doesn't fully work. Sleeping until noon on Saturday may make you feel better, but it shifts your body clock, which is why Monday mornings feel so painful.

So what actually helps? Experts mostly agree on a few simple habits: going to bed and getting up at roughly the same time every day, keeping the bedroom cool and dark, and putting screens away an hour before bed. If we treated sleep as seriously as diet and exercise, many of us would feel — and think — much better.

## Граматика
- conditionals — *If* we *treated* sleep as seriously as diet and exercise, many of us *would feel* much better. — Друга умова: уявна ситуація зараз. *If* + Past Simple, головна частина — *would* + дієслово. Минулий час тут не про минуле, а про нереальність.
- relative-clauses — this process, *which works* far less efficiently when we are awake, may help… — Неозначальне підрядне в комах додає інформацію. У ньому лише *which*, не *that*.
- present-perfect — A driver who *has been awake* for eighteen hours… — Стан, що триває до моменту, про який ідеться, → Present Perfect з *for*.
- gerund-after-verbs — *going* to bed and *getting up* at roughly the same time… *putting* screens away — Герундій як назва дії в переліку порад, як іменник: «лягати… вставати… відкладати».
