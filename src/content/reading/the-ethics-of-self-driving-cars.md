Few thought experiments have escaped the philosophy classroom as successfully as the "trolley problem". In its classic form, a runaway tram is heading towards five people; by pulling a lever, you can divert it onto a track where it will kill one. Should you? For decades, the question was largely academic. The arrival of self-driving cars appeared to change that, since someone, it seemed, would have to programme the answer in advance.

Public debate seized on the dilemma. Should a car swerve to avoid a group of pedestrians if doing so would endanger its passenger? Should it prioritise children over adults? Large online surveys asked millions of people to make such choices, and the results revealed striking differences between cultures.

Engineers working on autonomous vehicles, however, tend to regard these scenarios as something of a distraction. Situations in which a car must choose between two certain deaths are, they argue, vanishingly rare. Far more common — and far more consequential — are the mundane failures: misreading a faded road marking, failing to recognise a cyclist at dusk, braking too late on a wet surface. Were every such error eliminated, the number of lives saved would dwarf anything the trolley problem could decide.

There is also a practical objection. A car does not, in any meaningful sense, "know" that it faces a choice between five lives and one. It perceives probabilities: shapes that are likely to be people, trajectories that may or may not intersect. Any rule written in the language of moral philosophy would have to be translated into the language of uncertainty, at which point much of its clarity disappears.

None of this means that ethics is irrelevant to autonomous driving. On the contrary, ethical questions run through the whole enterprise: how much risk is acceptable before a system is released, who is liable when it fails, whose data is used to train it. These questions lack the drama of a runaway tram. They may, however, matter considerably more.

## Граматика
- conditionals-advanced — *Were every such error eliminated*, the number of lives saved *would dwarf* anything… — Умовна інверсія: *Were* + підмет + V3 замість *If every such error were eliminated*. Формальний академічний стиль.
- relative-clauses-advanced — the language of uncertainty, *at which point* much of its clarity disappears — *At which point* — «і тоді»: підрядне, що відсилає до всієї попередньої думки.
- distancing — Situations… are, *they argue*, vanishingly rare. — Вставне *they argue* — автор передає позицію інженерів, не беручи її на себе.
- emphasis — *Far* more common — and *far* more consequential — are the mundane failures — Інверсія з порівнянням на початку: присудок *are* стоїть перед довгим підметом, щоб наголосити на контрасті.
- modals-will-would-should — They *may*, however, *matter* considerably more. — *May* — обережне твердження, типове для висновку аналітичного тексту.
