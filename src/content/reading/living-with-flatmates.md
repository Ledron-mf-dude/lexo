When Ivanna moved to Kharkiv for her new job, she couldn't afford a flat on her own, so she found a room in a shared flat. There were four of them: Ivanna, a student called Dmytro, a nurse called Olha, and Ben, an English teacher from Manchester.

For the first month, everything was fine. Then the problems started. Dmytro never washed his dishes. Olha worked night shifts and needed to sleep during the day, but Ben practised the guitar every afternoon. And nobody ever bought toilet paper.

One evening, Olha called a flat meeting. "We have to make some rules," she said, "or I'm moving out."

They sat in the kitchen and agreed on a list. Everyone must wash their own dishes on the same day. There's a quiet time from ten in the morning until four in the afternoon. Everyone puts two hundred hryvnias a month into a shared box for cleaning products and toilet paper. And there's a cleaning rota: each person cleans the bathroom and the kitchen once a month.

Ben said that he would practise in the park instead. Dmytro promised that he would change, and, surprisingly, he did.

Six months later, the four flatmates are good friends. "Living with other people teaches you a lot," Ivanna says. "You learn to talk about problems before they become big ones. And you learn that you don't have to love everyone. You just have to respect them."

## Граматика
- modals-obligation — "We *have to make* some rules." … Everyone *must wash* their own dishes. — *Have to* і *must* — обов'язок. У правилах, які люди встановлюють самі, звучать обидва.
- modals-obligation — you *don't have to love* everyone. You just *have to respect* them. — *Don't have to* — «не обов'язково», а не «не можна».
- reported-speech — Ben *said that he would practise* in the park instead. — Непряма мова: *will* після *said* стає *would*.
- generic-pronouns — Everyone must wash *their* own dishes. — Після *everyone* — *their*, коли стать неважлива чи невідома.
