When the founders of a mid-sized design agency announced that their staff would work four days a week for the same pay, many of their clients assumed the company was in trouble. A year later, the agency is said to be more profitable than ever, and the experiment has become permanent.

The idea behind the four-day week is not simply to work less. It is to work differently. Before the switch, the agency spent months analysing how its employees actually used their time. The results were uncomfortable: on average, people spent almost a third of their week in meetings, many of which had no clear purpose, and a further chunk answering messages that could have waited.

So the rules changed. Meetings were limited to thirty minutes and required a written agenda. Two mornings a week were declared "deep work" time, during which notifications were switched off. Staff were encouraged to decline invitations to meetings where they were not genuinely needed.

The results, according to the company, have been striking. Projects are delivered on time more often, sick days have fallen by roughly forty percent, and the agency has had no difficulty attracting new designers. "People protect their Friday," one manager explains. "They're far more careful about wasting time on Tuesday."

Critics point out that the model does not suit every business. A hospital or a shop cannot simply close on Fridays, and in jobs where output is measured in hours rather than results, a shorter week usually means hiring more staff. Nevertheless, the experiment raises an uncomfortable question for many offices: how much of the traditional working week is genuinely productive, and how much is simply habit?

## Граматика
- passive-reporting-have-something-done — the agency *is said to be* more profitable than ever — *Be said to* + інфінітив — безособова передача чужих слів: «кажуть, що агенція…».
- past-modals — messages that *could have waited* — *Could have* + V3 — те, що було можливим, але не сталося: на ці повідомлення можна було відповісти пізніше.
- relative-clauses — meetings, *many of which* had no clear purpose — *Many of which / some of whom* — неозначальне підрядне про частину групи. Після прийменника — лише *which / whom*.
- passive-voice — Meetings *were limited* to thirty minutes… Staff *were encouraged* to decline… — Пасив у Past Simple: нові правила, головне — що змінилося.
- discourse-markers — *Nevertheless*, the experiment raises an uncomfortable question… — *Nevertheless* — «проте»: визнаємо критику, але повертаємося до головної думки.
