Few experiences are as reliably uncomfortable for an English speaker as a silence in conversation. A pause of more than about four seconds, research suggests, is enough to make most participants feel that something has gone wrong. Someone will rush to fill it — with a question, a joke, a remark about the weather — and the conversation will lurch on, relieved.

Not every culture shares this horror. In some, a pause before answering is a sign that the question has been taken seriously; to reply instantly might suggest that one has not really thought about it. In others, silence among friends is a mark of ease rather than awkwardness: only with strangers must one keep talking. Business negotiators who move between such cultures learn, sometimes expensively, that a silence they interpret as rejection may simply be consideration.

Even within a single language, silence carries meanings that words could not. A teacher who waits after asking a question, resisting the urge to answer it herself, communicates that she expects her students to think. A doctor who says nothing for a moment after delivering bad news allows the patient room to react. A negotiator who stays quiet after hearing an offer may well extract a better one, the other side hastening to fill the void with concessions.

What these examples have in common is that silence is not the absence of communication but a form of it — one that is, if anything, harder to master than speech. It requires one to tolerate a degree of discomfort, to resist the reflex to smooth everything over, and to trust that the other person will use the space.

It would be naive to recommend silence indiscriminately; there are pauses that wound as surely as words. But a speaker who can let a silence stand, rather than rushing to dispel it, has acquired something that no amount of vocabulary can supply.

## Граматика
- inversion-c2 — *only with strangers must one keep* talking — Інверсія після *only* + обставина на початку: *must one*. Безособове *one* — книжний стиль.
- absolute-clauses — the other side *hastening* to fill the void with concessions — Незалежний зворот із власним підметом пояснює, як саме досягається краща пропозиція.
- modality-c2 — A negotiator who stays quiet… *may well extract* a better one — *May well* — «цілком може»: вища ймовірність, ніж просте *may*.
- focus-adverbs — one that is, *if anything*, harder to master than speech — *If anything* — «якщо вже на те, то навіть…»: уточнює і посилює попереднє твердження.
- distancing — *research suggests* — Вставне посилання на джерело: *suggests* обережніше за *shows* чи *proves*. Автор дистанціюється від точної цифри.
