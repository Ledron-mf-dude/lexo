Of all the devices that scholarly writing has bequeathed to us, the footnote is perhaps the most modest and the most quietly subversive. Tucked away at the foot of the page, set in smaller type, it seems designed not to be read. And yet a great deal of intellectual history has taken place down there.

Its official function is straightforward enough: to show where a claim comes from, so that a reader who doubts it can check. In this sense the footnote is an instrument of honesty, a standing invitation to verify. A text without references asks to be trusted; a text with them asks to be tested. That the two make very different demands on their readers is not always appreciated.

But footnotes have rarely confined themselves to citation. Historians have long used them to conduct arguments too detailed, too technical or too impolite for the main text, so much so that one can sometimes reconstruct an entire scholarly feud from the small print alone. Others have used them for asides, qualifications and second thoughts — the places where an author, having made a confident statement above, quietly admits below that the matter is rather more complicated.

Then there are the jokes. A certain kind of writer treats the foot of the page as a backstage area where the formal tone can be dropped. Novelists, noticing this, have borrowed the form for fiction, producing books in which the footnotes gradually take over, commenting on, contradicting and eventually overwhelming the story they were meant to support.

Digital publishing has not been kind to the footnote. On a screen, it is often converted into a hyperlink or shunted to the end of the text, where few readers follow. Something is lost in the process. The footnote's power lay in its proximity: the main text and its doubts sharing a single page, each keeping the other honest.

## Граматика
- noun-clauses — *That the two make very different demands* on their readers *is not always appreciated*. — Підрядне з *That* у ролі підмета: дуже формальний стиль. Нейтрально: *It is not always appreciated that…*
- participle-clauses — an author, *having made* a confident statement above, quietly admits below… — Перфектний дієприкметник між підметом і присудком: дія, що сталася раніше.
- register-grammar — *Of all the devices* that scholarly writing has bequeathed to us, the footnote is perhaps the most modest — Винесення порівняльної групи на початок речення — риса письмового книжного стилю.
- comparison-idioms — *so much so that* one can sometimes reconstruct an entire scholarly feud from the small print alone — *So much so that* — «настільки, що»: підсилює попереднє твердження.
- absolute-clauses — the main text and its doubts *sharing* a single page, each *keeping* the other honest — Незалежні звороти з власним підметом (*the main text and its doubts sharing…*, *each keeping…*): стисло замість *and each of them keeps the other honest*.
